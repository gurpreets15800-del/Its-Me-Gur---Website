What Makes a Good Cup of Coffee?

A good cup of coffee is rarely about one perfect ingredient. It is the result of many small decisions coming together.

Coffee is often described through numbers — dose, yield, extraction time, temperature and grind size. All of these things matter, but they are only part of the story.

For me, a good cup starts with understanding what the coffee is trying to express. The origin, processing method, roast profile and freshness all create a foundation. Brewing then becomes the process of bringing that foundation into the cup.

Balance matters

One of the first things I look for in coffee is balance. Acidity, sweetness, bitterness, body and aroma should work together rather than one element completely dominating the experience.

This does not mean every coffee needs to taste the same. A bright and acidic coffee can be just as enjoyable as a heavier, chocolate-forward cup. The important question is whether the characteristics feel intentional and enjoyable.

Brewing is a conversation

The same coffee can behave very differently depending on how it is brewed. Grind size, water temperature, ratio, agitation and brewing time can all change what reaches the cup.

That is why I enjoy experimenting with different brewing methods. Brewing is less about following a recipe blindly and more about understanding what the recipe is doing.

The best coffee is the one you want to drink again

Technical accuracy is useful, but coffee is ultimately an experience. If a cup is perfectly extracted but nobody wants another sip, something is missing.

For me, good coffee sits somewhere between technique and enjoyment. It should make you curious about what is in the cup, while still being something you simply enjoy drinking.
