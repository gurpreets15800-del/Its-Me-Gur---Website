What I Learned From Opening a Café

Opening a café teaches you very quickly that an idea is only the beginning. The real work starts when people walk through the door.

Building a café has been one of the most practical learning experiences of my career. It brought together many things I had worked on separately — coffee, operations, products, people, costing and customer experience.

But running them together is completely different.

The idea is the easy part.

Before opening, everything exists as a plan. You think about the menu, the design, the equipment, the pricing and the experience you want to create.

Once the café opens, reality starts making decisions with you.

Customers tell you which products actually matter. Operations show you where your process is weak. Costing tells you whether an idea is commercially realistic. And small details that seemed unimportant during planning suddenly become part of the daily operation.

A café is a system.

One of the biggest lessons has been understanding how connected everything is.

A change in one recipe can affect food cost. A change in cost can affect pricing. Pricing affects customer perception. A new product affects inventory, training, preparation time and service.

Good products therefore cannot exist in isolation. They have to work for the customer and for the business at the same time.

You learn faster when you build.

There is a different kind of learning that happens when you are responsible for the outcome.

You stop looking at problems only from one perspective. You start thinking about the customer, the barista, the supplier, the numbers and the long-term sustainability of the business.

That perspective is something I want to carry forward into everything I work on — whether it is coffee, beverage innovation, product development or business.

— GURPREET SINGH
